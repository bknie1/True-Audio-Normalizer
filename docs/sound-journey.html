Across every kind of content

Four real, licensed recordings, four completely different mixing problems. Nothing is preloaded or autoplayed - press "Run through TAN" on each to hear the two-pass result for yourself, processed live in your browser.

Music

A comedian at a piano, 1966

Just voice and piano, recorded dry and close-mic'd, no post-production riding the fader. The dynamics are entirely human - a whispered aside, then a belted punchline, on purpose, because that's the joke. This is about as close to raw "as-performed" range as a music recording gets.

Movie

An animated short, full mix

Score, foley, and comedic sound effects all layered together - falls, impacts, and swelling music cues sitting right next to quiet character beats. This is the classic problem TAN exists for: the quiet parts and the loud parts were never meant to sit at the same volume, but your living room makes them.

Game

8-bit chiptune, synthesized

Square waves generated by code, not performed by anyone - there's almost no natural dynamic range here at all. This one is a different kind of demo: it shows TAN not over-correcting. Content that's already flat should come out basically untouched, and that restraint is as important as the leveling itself.

Podcast / spoken word

A real two-person interview

Unscripted conversation, no post-production riding the fader - one speaker leans into the mic, the other leans back, there's laughter and cross-talk. Nobody was evening this out in real time, which is exactly the kind of natural, human volume swing TAN is built to smooth over.
