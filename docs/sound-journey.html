Music

A mathematician's guide to plagiarism

Just voice and piano, recorded dry and close-mic'd, no post-production riding the fader. The dynamics are entirely human - a whispered aside, then a belted punchline, on purpose, because that's the joke. This is about as close to raw "as-performed" range as a music recording gets. Same performer, same piano - pick any track, the mixing problem is identical.

Movie

A 1968 horror trailer, mixed like 1968

Screaming stingers, blaring brass, and a narrator mixed hot, right up against dialogue and quiet dread that sit way down in the mono mix - decades before anyone normalized loudness at mastering time. This is the vintage-movie problem that started this whole project: the old stuff is exactly what modern gear handles worst.

Game

8-bit chiptune, synthesized

Square waves generated by code, not performed by anyone - there's almost no natural dynamic range here at all. This one is a different kind of demo: it shows TAN not over-correcting. Content that's already flat should come out basically untouched, and that restraint is as important as the leveling itself.

Podcast / spoken word

A real two-person interview

Unscripted conversation, no post-production riding the fader - one speaker leans into the mic, the other leans back, there's laughter and cross-talk. Nobody was evening this out in real time, which is exactly the kind of natural, human volume swing TAN is built to smooth over.
